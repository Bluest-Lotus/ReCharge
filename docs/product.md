# Phoenix Pack

Documentation maintained by the Project ReCharge team.

## 5. Phoenix Pack Product Architecture

### Product description

Phoenix Pack is a rechargeable powerbank concept using recovered lithium-ion cells where safety and qualification permit. Its purpose is to turn a difficult waste stream into a visible, useful demonstration of circular material recovery.

### Defined assembly parts

The revised prototype is divided into four main parts: bottom casing, top casing, circuit board and batteries. Cells are arranged in alignment to simplify robotic placement and electrical interconnection.

### Battery connections

The design direction uses friction- or pressure-fit electrical contacts rather than soldering directly to the cells, reducing assembly heat exposure. Contact resistance, retention force, vibration tolerance, oxidation, short-circuit protection and long-term reliability require validation.

### Unconfirmed specifications

Cell chemistry, dimensions, capacity, quantity, series/parallel arrangement, protection IC, charge/discharge limits, connector types, USB output ratings, thermal design and enclosure material are all To verify. No performance rating should be published until measured.

## 6. Electrical Design and Safety

### Electrical functions

The board design must address cell protection, overcharge/overdischarge, overcurrent, short circuit, temperature monitoring as appropriate, charging control and regulated output. Requirements depend on actual cell configuration and chemistry.

### Reuse qualification

A documented battery acceptance program should consider identity/chemistry, physical damage, voltage, capacity, internal resistance, self-discharge, history and compatibility. Thresholds must be defined by qualified engineering review and relevant standards—not inferred from this document.

### Design safeguards

Use insulating barriers, polarity-keyed placement, mechanically secure contacts, protected terminals, appropriate fusing/protection, strain relief, and enclosure features preventing access to energized conductors.

### Regulatory gate

Before any public lending, rental or sale, obtain specialist review of applicable Canadian electrical/product safety, lithium battery transport, recycling and consumer-product requirements, and appropriate independent testing/certification where required.

## 7. Mechanical Design

### Enclosure

The bottom casing locates and supports batteries and circuit board. The top casing closes and protects the assembly. CAD should include datums, fastening or snap-fit geometry, clearances, ventilation/thermal provisions and serviceability.

### Assembly-friendly design

Use consistent cell orientation, lead-ins, chamfers, locating bosses and accessible grasping surfaces. Avoid geometries requiring a robot to flex cells or force electrical contacts beyond validated insertion loads.

### Design outputs

Maintain controlled CAD files, exploded assembly, dimensioned drawings, material selection, tolerance stack-up, assembly instructions and engineering change records.

## 9. Verification and Quality Assurance

### Inspection stages

Incoming cell qualification; casing/PCB incoming inspection; part-presence and polarity checks; contact-engagement checks; enclosure inspection; electrical safety and functional tests; final traceability record.

### Test plan

Define test ID, purpose, fixture, instruments/calibration, sample size, acceptance limits, results, reviewer and corrective action. Candidate tests include contact resistance, output voltage, charging function, thermal behavior, drop/vibration, short-circuit protection and cycle endurance.

### Release control

No unit should enter student/public use without documented acceptance criteria, approved test results, safe-use instructions and product liability/regulatory review.
