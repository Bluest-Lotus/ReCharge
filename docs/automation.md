## 4. End-to-End Operating Model

### Collection

Receive intact devices in appropriately selected fire-resistant collection containers under a documented collection protocol. Label collection points and prevent contamination with unrelated waste.

### Intake and segregation

Record quantities, device condition and collection location. Quarantine visibly compromised items and use an approved recycler or qualified specialist for disposition.

### Recovery decision

Only qualified personnel following an approved battery-safety procedure should determine whether cells can be safely recovered and assessed. Reuse eligibility must not be assumed from visual condition alone.

### Disposition

Route rejected cells and other device fractions to appropriate licensed or otherwise verified recycling partners. Maintain custody and weight/count records.

### Manufacturing route

Only individually qualified, traceable cells would proceed to a validated powerbank build process. The manufacturing process is separate from hazardous-device dismantling.

## 8. Automation Engineering Approach

### Objective

Explore repeatable assembly of the four-part powerbank with robotic material handling, controlled placement and verification. The focus is design for automation, not the identity of individual team members.

### Proposed cell workflow

Present bottom casing in a repeatable fixture; locate casing; place prequalified cells in the aligned orientation; install the circuit board and engage validated pressure-fit contacts; fit top casing; inspect and transfer to an electrical test station.

### Equipment categories

Industrial robot or collaborative robot, end-effector/gripper, part feeders or trays, fixture/nest, PLC, discrete sensors, optional machine vision, guarding/interlocks, electrical test equipment and HMI. Exact models and installed devices remain To verify.

### Controls

PLC logic should coordinate station readiness, safe start, part-present signals, robot handshakes, fault states, recovery and test disposition. Robot programs should use validated frames, speed limits and collision checks.

### Major assembly challenges

Variation in reclaimed-cell size/condition; reliable orientation and grasping; consistent contact insertion force; electrical polarity verification; dimensional tolerance variation; enclosure closure; prevention of shorts; and dependable rejection/traceability.

### Safety boundary

Robot cell assembly should not be treated as permission to automate disassembly of hazardous disposable vape devices. Develop separate risk assessments and safeguards for each operation.

## 14. Schedule and Milestone Gates

### Gate A — baseline

Confirm product requirements, device/cell constraints, safety review and controlled design files.

### Gate B — prototype

Complete mechanical/electrical integration and documented bench validation.

### Gate C — automation

Demonstrate repeatable assembly of representative safe components with recorded cycle time, yield and fault recovery.

### Gate D — product qualification

Complete required safety, reliability and compliance assessment.

### Gate E — supervised pilot

Secure approvals, implement operating procedures, collect utilization and incident data.

### Gate F — expansion

Validate economics, supply and recycler capacity, maintenance and governance before scaling.
