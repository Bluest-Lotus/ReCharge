1. Executive Summary

Overview

Project ReCharge is a student-led circular-economy initiative addressing lithium-ion cells discarded in disposable vape devices. It combines responsible collection and battery recovery with research into safe reuse, recycling pathways and a prototype powerbank called the Phoenix Pack.

Mission

Reclaim lithium. Reduce waste. Recharge the future.

Current position

The initiative has demonstrated collection activity and a working powerbank prototype. A capstone automation effort is exploring repeatable assembly of a revised powerbank design. Product commercialization, deployment and automated production remain development goals rather than established operating capabilities.

Key objectives

Divert disposable vape devices from improper disposal; establish controlled handling and recycling routes; evaluate reclaimed cells for potential reuse; develop a manufacturable powerbank; test automated assembly; and build an economically and environmentally sustainable service model.

2. Problem Definition and Evidence

Waste stream

Disposable vape products contain lithium-ion cells alongside plastics, electronics and other components. Improper disposal can waste recoverable materials and create fire and environmental risks. Exact national disposal volumes and global recycling percentages cited in earlier project materials require source verification before external publication.

Local validation

An earlier student survey reported approximately 60% of respondents had used a vape and approximately 6% disposed of devices properly. Survey sample size, wording, collection period and denominator should be preserved with the raw results before using these figures in formal claims.

Collection evidence

A campus collection effort recovered more than 76 devices over three days. Retain dated collection logs, photographs, counts by category and final downstream disposition records to support this outcome.

3. Scope and Success Criteria

In scope

Awareness campaigns; controlled collection; sorting and inspection; battery characterization by trained personnel; feasibility evaluation; powerbank mechanical/electrical prototyping; robotic assembly concept; pilot business model; impact measurement.

Out of scope until validated

Unsupervised opening of damaged vape devices; consumer distribution of unqualified reused cells; claims of certification; automated cell recovery from vape housings without an independently reviewed hazard analysis; and broad commercial rollout.

Success criteria

Demonstrated safe collection and documented recycler handoff; reproducible engineering tests; an approved product design; reliable automated placement/assembly trials; traceable pilot metrics; and verified unit economics.

10. Environmental and Social Impact

Impact pathway

The initiative links responsible collection, material recovery, education and a tangible powerbank demonstration. Recycling remains the appropriate pathway for cells that cannot be responsibly reused.

Measures

Track devices collected, cell mass and count, proportion rejected, recycler handoff, cells qualified for reuse, powerbanks built/tested, collection engagement and educational reach. Avoid equating collection volume with verified emissions savings.

SDGs

Prior project materials identify contributions to multiple UN Sustainable Development Goals. Map each claimed SDG to a measurable project activity and supporting evidence before external reporting.
