## Automation 

### Overview

Project ReCharge is developing an automated manufacturing process for powerbanks made using lithium-ion batteries recovered from disposable vaping devices.

The system aims to integrate industrial robotics, machine vision, and automated assembly to improve manufacturing efficiency, consistency, and scalability.

### Design for Assembly (DFA)

The powerbank is designed around four primary components:

Bottom Casing: Supports and positions internal components.

Batteries: Reclaimed lithium-ion cells used for energy storage.

Circuit Board: Manages charging, protection, and power delivery.

Top Casing: Encloses and protects the assembly.

The batteries are aligned in a consistent orientation to simplify robotic placement. Friction-fit and pressure-fit contacts are being explored to establish electrical connections without directly soldering the cells, reducing heat exposure during manufacturing.

The casing and mounting features are designed to minimize assembly operations and improve positioning accuracy.

### Manufacturing Process

The proposed assembly process consists of three stages:

Battery Installation: Qualified lithium-ion cells are positioned into the mounting plate and connected through pressure-fit contacts.

Electronics Installation: The circuit board and display module, where applicable, are positioned and secured.

Enclosure Assembly: The internal assembly is enclosed within the top and bottom casing before inspection and testing.

### Materials and Components

The powerbank combines reclaimed lithium-ion cells with fabricated casing components, electrical contacts, a power management circuit board, and an optional display module.

Custom mechanical components can be prototyped using 3D printing to evaluate fit, alignment, and robotic handling before finalizing the design.

### Engineering Considerations

The primary challenge is accommodating variations in recovered batteries, including differences in dimensions, capacity, condition, and terminal configurations.

Other considerations include:

Safe handling, inspection, and qualification of reclaimed cells

Reliability of solderless electrical connections

Accurate positioning of mechanical and electronic components

Repeatability of robotic assembly

Inspection and functional testing of completed powerbanks

Recovered cells require appropriate qualification, and the completed powerbank must undergo electrical and safety validation.

### Development Status

Current Phase: Design and Development

The project is progressing toward an integrated robotic assembly system. Current development focuses on refining the powerbank design, establishing suitable handling methods, and evaluating equipment for assembly and inspection.
